# Competitive Positioning

## Reference Landscape

Atlas learns from strong projects without copying their product boundary.

### MITRE ATT&CK / ATT&CK STIX

Strengths:

- authoritative adversary knowledge model;
- structured STIX relationships;
- versioned machine-readable data.

Atlas differentiation:

- operational telemetry, event, detection, hunt, DFIR, and response context layered around adversary knowledge.

### SigmaHQ

Strengths:

- portable detection-rule ecosystem;
- shared detection vocabulary;
- validation and conversion tooling.

Atlas differentiation:

- Sigma is one representation attached to broader canonical entities and behaviors.

### Elastic Detection Rules

Strengths:

- detections-as-code lifecycle;
- schema validation;
- CLI and testing;
- vendor-native operationalization.

Atlas differentiation:

- vendor-neutral concept layer and cross-platform investigation graph.

### Splunk Security Content

Strengths:

- analytic stories;
- detections, playbooks, data sources, testing and packaging;
- clear operational workflows.

Atlas differentiation:

- not tied to one SIEM and not limited to SIEM content.

### Threat-Hunting Playbook Projects

Strengths:

- hypothesis-driven investigation;
- telemetry and hunt methodology.

Atlas differentiation:

- hunt content is connected to canonical events, artifacts, detections, controls, sources, and response actions.

## Strategic Gap Atlas Targets

Most existing projects optimize one major axis:

- threat knowledge;
- detection rules;
- vendor content;
- hunting notebooks;
- event references.

Atlas targets the **relationship layer across all of them**.

## Positioning Statement

> Cyber-Sentinel-Atlas is a vendor-neutral cyber defense knowledge graph and analyst workbench that connects telemetry, events, ATT&CK behavior, detections, hunts, DFIR evidence, defensive controls, and response guidance with claim-level provenance and offline-first access.

## Competitive Rule

Do not compete by having the largest raw content count.

Compete on:

- correctness;
- provenance;
- relationship quality;
- search speed;
- analyst utility;
- validation transparency;
- offline capability;
- product design.
